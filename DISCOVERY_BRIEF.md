# Fictional customer discovery brief

**Scenario:** A hypothetical small internal IT support team receives free-form requests about VPN, office Wi-Fi, account access and approved software. This is an authored scenario, not a real customer engagement; no ticket volume or time savings has been observed.

**User and pain:** A support specialist must identify the request, find applicable guidance and prepare a reply. Broad keyword matches can surface advice that does not address the request. Compromise reports require a specialist rather than a generic reset instruction.

**Current workflow assumed:** A person reads each ticket, looks up a local article and decides whether and how to reply. No enterprise ticketing system, identity provider or real knowledge base is connected.

**Constraints:** Fictional data only; no sending action; no secrets or external model calls by default; sensitive cases bypass optional model drafting; a person reviews every proposed answer.

**Success criteria for this prototype:** Correct category and relevant article or abstention on the authored cases; no generic article for credential compromise; every request pauses for approval, edit or rejection. The evaluation is a development check, not evidence of time saved or real-world accuracy.

**Next discovery with a real customer:** Observe request mix and escalation policy, define article ownership and freshness, establish permissions, gather consented historical examples, measure baseline handling time and error rates, and agree on a safe deployment environment. These steps are not yet completed.
